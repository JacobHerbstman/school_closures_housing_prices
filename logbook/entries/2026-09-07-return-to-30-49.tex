\section*{September 7, 2026: Return to the 30/49 comparison}
\textit{Codex draft recording Jacob's decision.}

Jacob chose to resume the original comparison of 30 treated and 49 control
schools selected from the February 2013 closure-consideration list.
Comparisons of homes at different distances remain separate diagnostics.
Their stricter exclusions had changed which school neighborhoods contributed
sales; those smaller samples will not define the main comparison.

The working sample includes sales within one-quarter mile of a treated or
control school. It excludes homes near both groups and homes within the same
radius of a school designated to receive displaced students (a welcoming
school) or another candidate school outside the selected groups. Homes near several
schools of the same status remain in the sample once, assigned to the nearest
site of that status. The citywide housing-price cleaning remains unchanged,
including the within-year upper price-per-square-foot trim.

School counts differ from the number of buildings and from the number of
school areas with eligible sales.
The 30 treated schools occupy 29 physical sites; the 49 control schools occupy
49 sites. The saved event-study sample contains 10,921 sales across 29 treated
and 48 control sites. Pooled comparisons omit 2013 and use 9,912 sales. The
return preserves both transaction and equal-site-period weights, school-site
and calendar-time fixed effects, and the existing housing-control comparisons.
It does not require sales in every distance band or adopt a new weighting rule.

The distance comparisons remain useful evidence about how exclusions change
the neighborhoods represented. The central research question remains whether
prices near the still-open schools show what would have happened near the
closed schools without closure. Differences in pre-closure trends and in the
mix of properties sold still require investigation. The annual raw trends and
weighting comparisons in the September 4 entry remain the starting exhibits.
