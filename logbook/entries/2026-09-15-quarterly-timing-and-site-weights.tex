Equal weights mean total weight one for each observed site-period. The observed
roster can change when a site has no sales. Equal site-quarter weights give a
smaller pooled dollar estimate, \$39,129 (95\% interval $-\$4,404$ to
\$82,661), because they redistribute influence differently from annual weights.
Positive pooled estimates do not resolve the pre-period movements or closure
selection. The original controlled annual log pre-test rejects (p=0.031).

\subsection*{Quarterly estimates do not locate a distinct announcement or closure effect}

Quarterly models use the full 2008--2018 sample with site and calendar-quarter
fixed effects, normalizing 2012 Q3 to zero. This displays 2012 Q4, containing
the December utilization list. The February candidate list and March proposals
share 2013 Q1; the May vote and June school-year end share Q2. Q3 is the first
full quarter after June. This frequency cannot separate the events within
either Q1 or Q2.

\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{../input/quarterly_price_events.png}

For transaction-weighted dollar OLS with controls, changes into 2013 Q1, Q2,
and Q3 are approximately \$34,258, $-\$25,026$, and $-\$22,426$.
Their intervals all include zero. This also holds for these three adjacent
changes in logs and PPML under all three comparisons. A Q4 rebound appears,
but quarterly sales and their composition do not identify its mechanism.
Treated sales number 30--45 per quarter in 2013, falling to 18--28 after the
site exclusions. Across the full period, 405 of 968 treated site-quarters
have no sales, and 217 of the 563 nonempty cells have one sale.

Joint tests for 2008 Q1--2012 Q2 relative to 2012 Q3 reject flatness in all
nine controlled quarterly event models (all p-values below 0.01). These are
diagnostics with many restrictions and limited clusters, not a remedy for
nonparallel trends. The imprecise 2013 changes also do not establish small
effects. Most early dates are recording months (correction, September 22: they are deed months; see ``What the sale dates measure''); from 2013 most are refined
instrument dates. Neither records when the transaction price was agreed.
Thus date coverage, transaction lags and thin samples limit timing claims.
