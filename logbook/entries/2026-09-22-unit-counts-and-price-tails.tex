\section*{September 22, 2026: Keeping apartment buildings with blank unit counts; flagging price tails}
\textit{Drafted by Claude; Jacob raised the unit-count concern and asked for the tail check.}

The price sample had required an observed unit count for every two-to-six-unit
building (class 211). This requirement removed 3,052 sales in 2008--2018,
8.2\% of eligible class-211 sales, but only 20 of 90,129 single-family sales
were lost to missing characteristics. The blanks are not random. Lake township
holds 43\% of them but only 16\% of other class-211 sales. The assessor's blank
share among class-211 records falls from 18\% in 2008 to 7\% in 2018. Blank-count
buildings sold for a median of \$135,000, against \$256,000 for other
class-211 sales. Their other records look like ordinary two- and three-flats.
Only 556 of 2,660 parcels report a count in any other year, and later records
are not copied backward. These sales now remain, with an unknown unit count.
The citywide sample rises from 124,396 to 127,446. The quarter-mile comparison
gains 135 treated and 239 control sales. Regressions that control for unit
count need an explicit unknown category.

After the foreclosure rule, little extreme low tail remains. The within-year 1st
percentile of nominal price is \$14,500--20,000. The lowest-priced sales mix
lenders missed by the name rule, such as Liquidation Properties and Household
Finance, with investor purchases of run-down buildings. Nearly a quarter of
these are resold within a year at a median of five times the price. Rather
than trimming, \texttt{price\_outside\_p01\_p99} flags sales outside the
within-year citywide 1st--99th price percentiles for the conventional
robustness check (2,444 sales). A price cutoff flags houses and apartment
buildings in proportion (0.9\% each). A per-square-foot cutoff would flag
2.8\% of apartment buildings but 0.3\% of houses. No regression has been rerun.
