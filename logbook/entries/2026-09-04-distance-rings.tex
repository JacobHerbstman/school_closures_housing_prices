\section*{September 4, 2026: Changing distance also changes the neighborhoods compared}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry; these comparisons remain exploratory.}

Jacob requested closer and farther rings with overlaps removed because the
choice of control neighborhoods and school-site fixed effects remained
unsettled. The new audit compares homes within one-eighth mile, between
one-eighth and one-quarter mile, and between one-quarter and one-half mile.
It also estimates cumulative quarter-mile and half-mile specifications.
The supplied 30 closed/49 open school split and citywide cleaning remain fixed.

For each band, a home must be within the specified distance of its nearest
study site and outside the outer radius of every other study site, including
sites with the same treatment status. Homes near welcoming or other candidate
sites are also excluded at that radius. This is stricter than the preceding
quarter-mile rule, which retained same-status overlaps. The stricter
quarter-mile sample removes 42 treated and 147 control sales, leaving 10,732.

The three distance bands contain, respectively, 1,239/2,168, 2,271/5,458, and
2,094/8,668 treated/control sales. The farthest band retains only 21 treated
sites, compared with 29 in each nearer band. Wider exclusions remove homes near
additional schools and substantial
portions of some neighborhoods. Consequently,
differences across these estimates mix distance with a changing comparison
sample; they are not a distance-decay estimate holding sites fixed.

All three site-weighted dollar estimates are positive but imprecise; all
six dollar/log intervals include zero. The event studies do not show a
clear effect that shrinks with distance. Pre-2013 movement remains visible,
particularly in log prices. Neither a nonsignificant pretest nor a wide
post-period interval establishes a valid counterfactual or a zero effect.

School-site fixed effects remove persistent differences between local housing
markets. They do not remove school-specific recovery paths. A diagnostic
replaces them with a single treated/control group intercept, retaining the
same housing controls, year effects, weights, and site-clustered inference.
The results remain imprecise. This does not support removing site fixed
effects as a solution to the comparison problem: doing so also exposes the
estimates to changes in which school areas supply sales.

The closest and farthest bands have some sites with no sales before or after
2013. Sites with observed sales remain in the analysis; empty years receive
no imputed prices. Equal site-year weighting therefore averages only sites
with transactions that year. A useful next diagnostic would hold the site
roster and exclusion radius fixed across bands before interpreting distance
patterns. This is a recommendation, not an adopted design. The distance
comparisons use housing characteristics as controls; amenity
distances and Census demographics are omitted from these models.
