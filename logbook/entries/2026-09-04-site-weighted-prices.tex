\section*{September 4, 2026: Equal site weights reduce the price estimate}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry; interpretation remains open for discussion.}

Jacob requested raw trends, DiD estimates, and event studies that give school
sites equal weight, alongside the existing transaction-weighted results.
The comparison uses the selected 30 closed and 49 still-open schools and
10,921 eligible sales within a quarter mile, at 29 treated and 48 control
physical sites. With annual time effects and housing
controls, the dollar DiD falls from about \$57,000 to \$11,500. The corresponding
log estimate moves from a 5.7\% increase to a 4.7\% decrease. Both site-weighted
intervals include zero.

{\footnotesize Pooled DiDs compare 2008--2012 with 2014--2018, omitting 2013.
Brackets contain pointwise 95\% intervals clustered by school site. Dollar
outcomes use 2022 purchasing power. Log coefficients and their endpoints are
converted using $100[\exp(\beta)-1]$. The amenity specification also includes
hedonics and home distance measures interacted with each half-year.}

Each sale receives weight one divided by the number of eligible sales at its
school site in the same year or half-year. The weights sum to one within
an observed site-period. The raw curves consequently average prices within
each site-period and then across sites. This differs from the earlier
cross-sectional comparison, which pooled all pre-2013 sales within each site
before averaging sites. Weights are not based on prices or square footage.

The raw curves show substantially smaller treated/control gaps under equal
site weights, especially in logs. The event studies also lose much of the
persistent positive dollar difference in later years. The estimates therefore
depend strongly on how much weight high-volume
school neighborhoods receive. Neither weighting rule by itself establishes
that the comparison identifies the effect of closure. The
semiannual hedonic dollar DiD falls from about \$57,800 to \$18,300. With
amenity-by-half-year controls it falls from \$58,500 to \$30,800, also with an
interval that includes zero. Census demographic controls have not been added.

Equal weights do not uniformly improve the pre-period evidence. The annual
uncontrolled dollar pretest p-value increases from 0.028 to 0.373, while the
annual hedonic log pretest changes from 0.700 to 0.359. Both weighted outcomes
retain some pre-closure movement. Differences around 2010 and the recovery
before 2013 remain relevant; failure to reject a joint test does not establish
parallel trends.

There are 26 empty site-years out of 847 possible cells and 109 empty
site-half-years out of 1,694. No price is imputed, and the full sales sample
remains intact. Equal weights apply to sites with sales in a given period;
the contributing roster varies. Low-volume sites have greater influence, and
weights cannot remove within-site selection into selling or changes in the
kinds of homes sold. Annual results provide the less sparse first comparison.
