\section*{September 7, 2026: Initial-price adjustment in the log models}
\textit{Codex draft; sensitivity check requested by Jacob.}

Adding the same initial-price-by-year interactions to the log models lowers
the pooled coefficient with property controls from 0.0552 to 0.0320.
Exponentiating gives 5.7\% and 3.2\%, respectively; the adjusted 95\%
interval is --12.0\% to 21.1\%. These percentages describe the conditional
geometric-mean contrast, not a percentage change in arithmetic mean prices.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\hline
Pooled log coefficient & Existing model & Initial price $\times$ year added \\
\hline
Site and year fixed effects & 0.0744 & 0.0547 \\
With property controls & 0.0552 & 0.0320 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Only the outcome changes to log price. The initial-price covariate remains
the site's pooled 2008--2012 arithmetic mean price in dollars, divided by
\$100,000, interacted with calendar year. It is not itself logged. The sample,
transaction weights, property controls, and school-site clustering are
unchanged. The annual models use 2012 as reference; pooled models omit 2013.

The annual log estimates remain close to zero and do not develop the sustained
post-closure rise seen in the dollar specifications. With property controls,
the adjusted 2016, 2017, and 2018 coefficients are --0.0243, 0.0330, and
0.0419; all pointwise 95\% intervals include zero. Initial-price adjustment
therefore does not resolve the difference between the two outcome scales.
This remains an exploratory comparison, with the same noisy initial means and
conditional inference described in the preceding dollar adjustment.
