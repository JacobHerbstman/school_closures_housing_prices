\section*{September 22, 2026: What the sale dates measure; split buildings}
\textit{Drafted by Claude; checked at Jacob's request before regressions.}

Earlier entries described pre-2013 sale dates as recording months. That is
wrong. The recorder document number encodes the recording date: year, day of
year, and a sequence number. Among sales without a refined MyDec date, the
recording month equals the \texttt{sale\_date} month for 29\% of sales. It is
one month later for 51\% and two months later for 12\%. Refined MyDec dates
precede recording by a median of 15 days, and 90\% fall within 63 days. The
pre-2013 date is therefore the deed month with its day set to 1, as the
Assessor's metadata describe for its older ingest process. The date measures
the same event, the deed or closing, in both periods. Only its precision
changes, from month to day. Annual, half-year and quarterly assignment is
exact in both periods. The master now labels these dates \texttt{sale\_month}
rather than \texttt{recording\_month}. The label is the only change to the
master data.

A separate timing caveat remains. Neither date records when the price was
agreed, usually a month or two before closing. Sales closing in April or May
2013 were often under contract before the March 21 proposal. Timing analyses
should allow for this lag rather than treat the closing date as the date of
price formation.

The clean sample also now excludes 283 sales, 0.2\% of the sample, whose parcel
holds only part of a building (a tieback proration below 1, usually one half).
Their assessor characteristics describe the whole building, a median of 2,080
square feet, while the sale covers one parcel. Their price per square foot is
correspondingly low: a median of \$85, against \$124 for other sales. The
rule parallels the single-building-card requirement. The citywide 2008--2018
sample is now 167,029 sales. After the master rebuild, the county downloads
re-ran and again matched the September 7 checksums. One coordinate request
failed transiently on the first attempt; the previous file was kept, and the
retry succeeded.
