# Foreclosure-related sales near closure candidates

Compare annual seller-name proxies and reported deed types near the 30 treated programs and 49 comparison schools. The primary population is the existing 2008–2018 single-family and townhouse price packet within half a mile, with its treatment definitions, geographic exclusions, cleaning and transaction weights unchanged. A second population keeps the same geography and single-family/single-card definition before price and county-quality screens. It still excludes the master file's multi-parcel and condominium transactions.

Run `make` in `code/`. The three-page `output/distress.pdf` shows seller proxies, reporting coverage and deed shares, and the comparison before price screens. `output/distress.rds` retains transaction indicators and supporting tables; `report/distress.txt` describes them and records source hashes.

The narrow proxy matches sellers named as judicial-sale companies or sheriffs, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or HUD. The broader proxy adds bank and mortgage-company names. Matching rules, including abbreviations, are in `code/seller_patterns.csv`. These measure recorded seller identities, not independently verified foreclosure status. Bank sellers can be land trustees; short sales and later resales by private investors can go undetected. A company buyer is not automatically an investor or a distressed purchase, and no investor classification is made here.

The primary plotted denominator is transactions with a usable seller name. Missing names are not classified as non-distressed. All-transaction match shares and bounds for unknown names are retained as well; those bounds do not address false positives or unrecognized names. The 2015 proxy points are omitted because missing/UNKNOWN seller names make comparisons especially unreliable. All coverage and counts remain available. Detailed MyDec deed types are almost absent before 2012; deed shares are shown only from 2013, separately from name proxies.

The audit reuses the saved county sales extract, including `sale_seller_name` that production price data do not retain. It does not refresh sales. The public [Parcel Sales metadata](https://datacatalog.cookcountyil.gov/api/views/wvhk-k5uv.json), first retrieved September 15, 2026 and preserved locally, exposes no separate REO, short-sale or investor field. The county's [internal view definition](https://github.com/ccao-data/data-architecture/blob/1a64383a9a4b1791f4aa5ecc71841f6006184350/dbt/models/default/default.vw_pin_sale.sql) includes additional MyDec questions that are not fields in this public export.

Seller roles are supported by [The Judicial Sales Corporation](https://www.tjsc.com/), [Fannie Mae's REO sale guidance](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/5291/display), and [HUD's REO program](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/reo-management). Those institutional roles motivate the proxies; they do not validate the status of individual transactions. These descriptive comparisons cannot establish that foreclosure recovery explains, or does not explain, the price pre-trends.
